About the conference

Great Lakes AI Week is a regional artificial intelligence conference produced by the Enterprise Technology Association. It brings AI leaders from Cleveland, Toledo, Detroit, Pittsburgh and other cities to Northwest Ohio.

The program includes keynote speakers, fireside chats, panels, workshops, breakout sessions and networking. Organizers expect more than 70 speakers. An expo features AI vendors, startups and technology partners.

Before you go

Parking
Free on both campuses. Detailed parking instructions will be sent before the event.
Arrival
Arrive early to allow time for parking and check-in.
What to wear
Business casual. November weather in Northwest Ohio can change quickly, so bring layers.

Meet employers

Many companies will take part in the conference, from major technology companies such as Tesla, NVIDIA, Google, Anthropic and Microsoft to regional IT companies. The conference is an opportunity to meet their representatives and learn how they use AI.

Details about a talent fair and experiential learning opportunities are still being finalized and will be shared when available.

Volunteer at the conference

Volunteers help run the conference. They check in attendees, keep sessions on time, guide people between rooms and buildings, support sponsors and speakers, and help with lunch service.

Volunteering supports BGSU and the regional community, and it is a good way to meet professionals who work with AI.

Student volunteers did excellent work at last year's conference. At least one BGSU student volunteer was later hired by a participating employer after meeting them through the conference.

How volunteering works

  1. Complete the Great Lakes AI Week volunteer form.
  2. Attend one orientation and training call. The calls cover the volunteer roles and how the conference days run.
  3. Read the volunteer guide. Volunteers receive a full guide with role descriptions and shift details. Setup and teardown times are being finalized with the universities and will be included in the guide.
